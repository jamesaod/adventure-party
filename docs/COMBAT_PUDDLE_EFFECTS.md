# Combat puddle splashes and ripples — R238C422

Combat-only extension of R238C420 basins. Rain produces small ballistic droplet bursts plus expanding, fading surface rings. Impact rate is proportional to squared rain intensity (maximum eighty impact opportunities/second), with at most eight candidate puddle samples per opportunity. Only outdoor grass-basin faces with standing water greater than two millimetres qualify. Existing friendly visibility gates impact generation; ripples are clipped by the existing terrain shader's actual puddle mask and inherit floor lighting.

Walking splash bursts use visible living units' interpolated world-space ground positions and a 0.45 m stride-distance threshold, with alternating lateral offsets. This supports ordinary combat actor presentation without per-bone sampling. Idle poses and camera translation cannot accumulate stride distance; jumps greater than two metres reset the stride accumulator. Corpses are excluded. This does not alter movement costs or terrain state.

Budgets: 96 live rings and 128 live droplet particles. Rings are uniforms in the existing terrain draw; droplets share one reusable points buffer and at most one extra draw before actors/ground cover. No reflection target, per-droplet meshes, new scheduler or added floor tessellation. Candidate selection is bounded; eligibility checks use cached face geometry. Rain and foot effects fade out in under 1.1 seconds. Drying continues through the existing weather scheduler, which temporarily uses normal frame pacing while effects remain active. Snow coverage suppresses water effects. Scene changes clear particles, rings and movement contacts; renderer disposal releases GPU resources.

Known limits: foot bursts follow stride distance, not exact skeletal sole contacts. The source rain VFX is a separate screen-space presentation; these are statistical water impacts, not collision tests against individual falling rain streaks. Droplets use a restrained fixed water tint rather than reflective materials. Basin geometry and hydrology retain the limitations documented in COMBAT_WET_BASINS.md. Visual/performance QA belongs to the user; fixed budgets do not constitute a hardware FPS guarantee.

Validation: 122/122 combat regressions passed. Targeted tests cover stride triggering, stationary/large-jump behavior, dry/indoor/stone exclusion, heavier rain frequency, particle/ring bounds, expiry, snow suppression and scene reset. Browser combat smoke checked shader compilation, heavy-rain impacts, camera pans without phantom footsteps and effect expiry after rain stops. Two pre-existing optional prepared-unit appearance cache misses fell back to generated appearance; no terrain/effect asset or shader failed.

The browser also drove an actual rendered player's walk presentation across a sampled puddle: one stride burst registered, camera-only movement registered zero, and all particles/rings expired after rainfall stopped. No shader/runtime errors occurred.

## R238C422 — rain visibility and 8x ripples

Rain impact opportunities increase from 10 to 80 per second at heavy intensity; the squared intensity response is unchanged. The per-frame catch-up budget rises from two to eight and ring capacity from 12 to 96, preserving ring lifetime and expansion speed instead of clipping away the requested frequency. Actual impacts still require eligible standing water. The 128-droplet budget remains unchanged. Terrain shading rejects rings outside a fragment's bounding square before evaluating the radial wave; there is no extra draw or full-screen pass.

The old rain rebound peaked at 0.028 metres (about 0.28 logical vertical pixels), explaining its subtle appearance. Rain droplets now peak at 0.253 metres, live 0.65 seconds, spread farther and have brighter, more opaque highlights. Point diameters now account for the framebuffer-to-logical-view density instead of shrinking on the game's supersampled canvas. Walking velocity and stride timing remain unchanged. Falling rain remains an overlay; water impacts are independently sampled, not literal collision events from those streaks.

R238C422 verification: 122/122 combat regressions passed. The all-water deterministic test produced exactly eight impacts per 100 ms heavy-rain tick. Headless combat compiled the larger shader successfully, displayed 48–52 active rings and 56–62 live droplets during sampled rain/walking frames, triggered a walking splash, produced no false footfalls during camera panning, and expired all effects after rainfall stopped. No shader/runtime errors occurred; the same two optional prepared-unit cache misses used their established fallback. Hardware FPS and visual appearance remain user QA.
