# TreeIt firs in the grassy battlefield test

Five TreeIt fir variants are centered on decorated grass hexes with stable seeded rotations. The test map now paints outdoor ambient light at 250% (previously the default 100%).

`combat-fir-trees.mjs` loads the exported model geometry and original shared textures. It produces transparent GPU renders for the existing foot-sorted combat compositor. Results are cached by tree variant, rotation, sun time, ambient setting and camera orientation; there is no extra animation loop. Sun or camera changes regenerate the needed views.

The foliage preserves the preview's green tint, second rotated layer, canopy-depth shading and one-third received shadow strength. Hemisphere illumination and canopy fill respond to ambient light and the solar clock. Ground shadows project the actual branch and foliage-card geometry along the current sun direction, merge its silhouette, and soften its edges. Sun shadows disappear at night. These renders provide self-shading; tree-to-tree shadows and local torch/spell illumination on tree bodies are not included in this pass.

The original body and shadow atlases remain as a fallback. Body capture dimensions are 384×512 with trunk anchor 192,448, a vertical span of 210 model units, and display height 240 tactical pixels. Authored size differences are retained. Ground shadows draw before actors; tree bodies retain the existing actor order. Gameplay LOS, targeting and tree coverage are unchanged.

The native TRE projects, OBJ/MTL exports, GLBs and preparation scripts are preserved in the development workspace under work/treeit-firs and work/treeit-preview. Seeds are 134, 961, 497, 567 and 953. Consult SAMPLE-LICENSE.txt and ATTRIBUTIONS.md before distributing artwork.

Validation: repository and combat renderer/environment checks; browser launch through the common combat initializer; morning/noon/afternoon/night visual checks. A fixed-view pixel check measured mean opaque tree brightness increasing from 58.7 at 100% ambient to 101.6 at 250%; nighttime produces no sun shadow. No browser script or shader errors were observed.

## Live local-light experiment

Tree bodies now rerender their mesh materials when their nearby light inputs change. Up to four strongest reachable torch/unit/spell lights are transformed into tree-relative 3D point lights. Existing CPU light reach remains authoritative, so blocked sources are excluded. Ground shadows remain cached; changing local light does not rebuild them. Separate per-hex caches prevent one instance from reusing another instance's lighting. Unchanged inputs reuse the body image, and no separate frame loop is added.

Canopy exposure now attenuates indirect illumination (12% in the deepest interior to full at the outside), with a reduced supplemental fill. This is a radial canopy approximation, not baked ray-traced ambient occlusion. Direct sunlight and local point lights remain independent of that attenuation.

This deliberately retains the hybrid compositor: meshes are rendered live offscreen and then inserted in actor order. It is not a shared-scene mesh migration. Local lights do not cast new tree shadows; cross-tree shadows remain deferred. The four-light cap bounds work, but moving/flickering lights do cause extra mesh renders and need user performance QA. `combatFirTrees.setLiveLighting(false)` provides a solar-only comparison; reenter combat after switching to redraw.

Browser checks confirm colored local light changes tree pixels, 250% ambient still brightens the canopy, and nighttime omits sun shadows. Daytime battlefield inspected visually. User performs final appearance/performance QA.

Bark adjustment: multiply the shared opaque trunk/branch material albedo by 0.45 once at load. Foliage tint is unchanged. Browser checks verified torch color/flicker reaches the local-light adapter and colored point illumination changes the rendered tree pixels.


Lantern correction: the ground/wall party lantern comes from explorationVisualLights, not illuminationSources. Trees now consume that physical light list with its metre position, height, range, intensity and decay, plus magical sources. Convert metre-based intensity into model units using the source decay exponent. A nighttime check with the actual party-lantern inputs increased mean opaque tree brightness from 0.22 to 68.2; previous synthetic-light checks did not cover this missing input.

Foliage highlight ceiling: after tone mapping and output encoding, cap foliage RGB at (0.36, 0.72, 0.24). Bark retains its separate dark material. This deliberately limits colored highlights to a green foliage ceiling. Combat-test fir hexes are generated once per session into a movement-only obstacle set; full-footprint standing and pathfinding reject them. Spawn footprints have persistent scenery clearance. Trees do not become architectural walls or change LOS. Browser verification found 265 blocked fir hexes over the full test map, rejected standing/path entry, and confirmed all initial units clear of those hexes.

Spell-light correction: use the shared lantern metre-to-model intensity/decay conversion for magical tree lights, with transient spell emitters at 2.2m instead of a low 28-model-unit height. The former inverse-square scaling severely underlit the canopy. Production animateFireballExplosion browser check sampled nighttime tree brightness before/during/after at 46.54/53.22/46.54 with the party lantern retained. The actual Fireball emitter, envelope and expiry were exercised. Foliage highlight ceiling remains active.

Fireball combustion test: blast cells ignite healthy fir obstacles once. Per-session firFireStates records burning until the next round boundary, then permanently burnt. Burning trees draw animated flames in the existing tree actor pass and contribute flickering orange light through illuminationSources, reaching floors/walls/other trees. The existing shared transient-effects scheduler animates them; no independent loop. Burnt models hide both foliage layers and use charcoal bark, with separate cached views and branch-only ground shadows. Obstacles remain. Recasting does not reset burnt trees. There is no fire spread or extra creature damage in this experiment. Browser production-explosion test verified ignition/light, round expiry, continued collision, foliage alpha-pixel reduction from 14745 to 2128, and regenerated bare shadows.

GPU flame follow-up: burning lasts until ignition round +2. Replace flat flame shapes with one reusable 192-point GPU pool, sampling actual mesh vertices per variant. Vertex shaders animate upward travel, curling drift and seeded phase; fragment shaders draw tapered soft yellow/orange flames and smaller embers. Point depth testing retains foliage occlusion; the existing compositor retains actor ordering. Per-tree particle allocation shares a 2048-particle budget across burning trees. No smoke or new particle loop. Shadow captures exclude the flame pool. Browser test verified GPU compilation, production ignition, survival at first round boundary, bare/charred transition at second, and continued movement blocking; sampled flame render inspected.

Burning appearance adjustment: a shader uniform halves foliage display RGB during burning (equivalent to a 50% black overlay; alpha stays opaque). Per-tree firelight maximum intensity is halved from 1.8 to 0.9. Seeded sinusoidal weights mix white, yellow and orange with independent frequencies/phases per tree; intensity also pulses smoothly. Fire/spell floor overlay samples each fragment's owning hex instead of its centroid, eliminating fragment-dependent brightness boundaries across open hexes; architectural emitter clipping remains. Ambient and physical-light exposure retain their existing paths.

Burning-color correction: removed the post-lighting 50% shade multiplier. Burning foliage now has a black base material color, retaining the material lighting pipeline and opaque leaf coverage. Healthy material color restores for other instances. Firelight palette is red/yellow/orange, replacing white; half-strength emission and two-round duration remain.
