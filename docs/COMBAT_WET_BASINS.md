# Combat dirt depressions and rain — local R238C420

Production combat only; neither map editor nor the isolated rain lab is migrated. Existing world-anchored worn grass patches become tapered basins up to 0.1524 m (six inches) deep. Their original texture mask is unchanged. A static RGBA field packs wear, depth and slopes once at renderer startup. Floor vertices use shallow downward displacement; grass, rocks and sticks use the same depth field. Outdoor grass floor retains its existing tessellation; existing snow refinement remains at 8x. Fine basin relief comes from the static field, while geometric displacement is limited by the existing vertex spacing. Indoor surfaces, stone, roads and walls are excluded. Movement, LOS and tactical elevations are unchanged.

The existing weather controls drive a bounded four-Hz soil/water state. Heavy rain darkens dirt, saturates it, then raises a shallow water plane from basin bottoms. Water is capped at basin capacity; excess is discarded as drainage rather than spreading into adjacent terrain. Clear weather drains puddles before drying the remaining mud. The existing weather scheduler continues at four Hz during clear-weather drying, then stops. No new animation loop, render target or separate water pass. Hidden time does not accumulate rain; a new scene starts dry. Snow still composites over the wet ground and retains the 12-inch cap.

This first version represents similarly compacted dirt basins with one shared saturation/water state, not individually simulated catchments. Rates are deliberately accelerated presentation seconds. There is no between-basin runoff, soil churn, muddy footprints, movement penalty, save-state persistence or precise actor-foot height adjustment. Trees, actors and projected shadow receivers retain their established ground anchors; the shallow offset is at most 1.524 presentation pixels before zoom. Basin normals use baked relief shading, not a new dynamic shadow pass. Cover roots follow the full static depth field; floor shape is an approximation at the chosen tessellation density.

Performance costs: wear texture changes from one to four channels (approximately 4 MiB rather than 1 MiB), cached cover-root heights packed in the existing instance records (no added cover texture lookup), low-resolution floor displacement, and existing-shader wetness/puddle blending. No per-frame terrain simulation grid or texture upload. No extra terrain triangles are retained; an experimental 2x subdivision added about 1.8 ms to the panning CPU benchmark and was discarded. No claim of zero FPS cost. User owns visual/performance QA.

Validation: `node scripts/test-combat-wet-basins.mjs` checks depth limits, center-first pooling, heavy versus light rain, puddles-before-mud drying, snow exclusion, bounded update timing, hidden time and scene reset. Full combat regressions and browser results are recorded in the delivery.

## Final verification

121/121 combat regression checks passed. Headless Chrome rendered the production scene without shader or runtime errors, filled the rain reservoir to 0.1524 m under accelerated heavy rain, then drained it to 0.0544 m while saturation remained 1. Two optional prepared-unit appearance cache requests returned 404 and fell back to generated appearances; no terrain resource failed.

Matched fixed-seed dry-scene panning benchmark (1600x1000, 10 warmup frames and 35 measured frames per condition): original CPU frame submission 24.49 ms, retained version 24.93 ms; stationary 14.87 to 15.28 ms. This single-run CPU test does not establish hardware GPU/FPS cost. Grass geometry was unchanged (3,272 instances, 96 draws and 1,256,120 triangles during the pan). Extra floor subdivision was removed after measuring 26.34 ms with that experiment. Production browser shader checks also ran on the final unchanged-topology version. Visual QA remains with the user.
