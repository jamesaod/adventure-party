# The Bells of Blackwater

Original setting concept for an Adventure Party campaign; saved for future development, not implemented content. Party size and composition remain undecided.

Eighty years ago, the royal city of Veyr disappeared beneath a new lake. Nobles blamed a rebellion; temples blamed the king. Now the water is receding. Old roads emerge, drowned steeples become visible, and bells ring beneath the water. Villagers wake with wet feet after dreaming of places they have never visited.

The party arrives in Brackenford, a shabby frontier town becoming a salvage boomtown. Divers recover royal silver, merchants purchase dubious claims, and descendants of dispossessed nobles demand their estates back. The town council is increasingly bought and paid for.

The opening job is modest: escort a clerk to an exposed tollhouse and verify ownership. Soldiers already occupy it. They are neither ghosts nor undead. They insist the king is alive, the year is eighty years earlier, and the clerk's paperwork is evidence of treason.

## Places and tensions

- **Brackenford:** temples, training, rival salvage companies and competing patrons.
- **The Reed March:** flooded farms, giant insects, smugglers and established human and orc communities.
- **Saint Orra's Causeway:** exposed in the morning and submerged by evening; something follows travelers beneath the water.
- **The Crown Mines:** dwarven records contradict both the royal and temple accounts of the disaster.
- **Veyr:** some districts have aged eighty years; others are still living through the city's final week.

Upland orcs have lived here for generations, with farms, shrines and fortified towns. Returning human claimants call them invaders. Some chiefs seek recognition; others prepare for war. Neither side is a single faction.

## The buried truth

Veyr was deliberately drowned to contain something beneath the palace. Seven bells hold the prison closed. Salvagers have already removed two. Restoring them may save the frontier while condemning the surviving people of Veyr again. Breaking the prison could rescue those people and finish the war that began eighty years ago.

Narrow causeways, flooded courtyards, docks and rooftops provide varied tactical battlefields. The recurring image is a church bell hanging from a salvage crane, dripping lake water—and ringing without moving.
